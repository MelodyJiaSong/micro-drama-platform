## 驳回
- a l03-l07 (primary rewrite): it's still a table of contents ("First… Then… A new way… And finally…"), and every line is longer than the original (l03 goes from 3 to 6 words, l04 from 4 to 5), which breaks the V6 length rule. a's alternative two-sentence fold reads well, but it drops the compression-lens item and leaves 2 lines for a 4-item card. Use the rewrite in 补充问题.
- b l03-l07: "Let's see / what goes wrong / when models memorize answers, / and what they could learn instead." splits one sentence across separately voiced lines with 0.3-0.4s pauses, so the choppy, card-driven rhythm stays. It also turns item 3 (compression, a different way to think about learning) into "what they could learn instead", which isn't what that chapter covers.
- a l14: "Its error is how far the points sit from the line, on average." quietly switches the measure from mean squared error (the scene counts `mse()`; see scenes/web/s3.html) to average distance. That makes l21's "over twenty times model two's" wrong, since a squared-error ratio of about 20x is only about 4-5x without squaring. b's diagnosis of l14 is right, but its rewrite ("we square each point's vertical distance… then average the results") adds math load for this audience. Keep the meaning and move the formula to the end of the line: "Model one is too simple: just a straight line. We score it by how far the points miss the line, squared and averaged."
- a l59: "…try a question you've never seen. And next time, we'll look at reinforcement learning." complains about the repeated "Next", then repeats "next time" itself, and it's longer than the original. Better: "…try a question you've never seen. Up next: reinforcement learning."
- b l43: "…against about eleven hundred flu measurements" removes "data points", but l44 says "With far more terms than data points", so its back-reference would point at nothing. a's l43 rewrite keeps the phrase and is the better choice.
- b l50: "Those models took shortcuts" drops "last two". Google Flu (l42-l45) is in the same chapter and was an overfitting case, not a shortcut case, so "Those models" would wrongly include it.
- b l09: "A model can learn random quirks in its practice data. Those quirks trip it up on new data." drops "fits its practice data so closely", the mechanism the episode's definition and its one-sentence takeaway depend on. It also adds "quirks" as another word for noise. a's version (just drop "First, the idea.") is the better fix.
- b l49: "seems to have learned that clue" has no antecedent, because the ink marks are never called a clue in the rewrite. The em-dash aside "melanoma—a skin cancer—" also keeps a nested structure in a line flagged for nesting. Use a's l49 rewrite ("Then there's a skin-cancer detector.
