About Changsha IFS and the Kaws Sculpture

1. Historical Origins: Two Towers That Replaced a Skyline

Changsha’s skyline was flat for most of its history. The city was rebuilt from ash in 1938, when a fire burned most of it to the ground, and again in the 1990s — and what grew back was low, dense and horizontal. Changsha IFS, completed in 2017, is the point at which that changed. At 452 metres to the top of the tower, it is the tallest building in central China, and it arrives as a pair: two identical towers joined by a podium that fills a whole city block above Wuyi Square.

The podium is the useful part. Below the towers sits the mall, running from luxury flagships on the ground floor to a food hall higher up, and the roof between the towers is an open-air terrace rather than a sealed climate zone. That is unusual in China, where malls tend to be sealed boxes, and it is the reason the building appears in almost every photograph of the city: you can stand on the seventh-floor terrace, look up between the towers, and get the whole thing in one frame.

2. What to Spot: KAWS, the Terrace, and the Light at Dusk

🧸 SEEING/WATCHING

  • The Story: The two enormous KAWS figures — one seated, one standing, both with the X-ed out eyes — were installed in 2018 as a permanent commission, and they are sculptural rather than decorative: 8 metres of bronze-cast cartoon, wedged into the gap between the towers.
  • What to Look For: The scale against the people on the terrace is the point. They sit on the roof of the podium, visible from Wuyi Square below and from the seventh-floor sculpture court at eye level.

🌇 The Seventh-Floor Terrace

  • The Story: An open-air sculpture court on top of the mall, free to enter, with the towers rising directly out of it.
  • What to Look For: Late afternoon. The sun sets down the gap between the two towers, which is the shot everyone comes for; go up around an hour before sunset and stay for the lights coming on.

🛍️ The Podium

  • The Story: The mall below the towers, including the Hunan debut stores of most major international brands, arranged in a spiral that keeps you moving rather than lost.
  • What to Look For: The food floors near the top — better value than the luxury below, and air-conditioned on a hot Changsha afternoon.

3. Cultural Contrast: A Mall as a Public Viewpoint

In most Chinese cities a skyscraper of this size would have a paid observation deck on the top floor, with a turnstile and a price. Changsha IFS does not. The towers are offices and a hotel; the public part is the podium and its roof, and the best view in the building costs nothing to reach.

The trade-off is that you will be looking at a shopping centre, not at a monument. That is not a criticism — Changsha’s centre of gravity really is retail, and the IFS podium sits in the middle of a walking district that runs from Wuyi Square through Huangxing Road to Pozi Street. Treat the building as the landmark you navigate by rather than the destination, and the evening works out better: walk the terraces, then eat 400 metres south where the food is cheaper and better.

4. Practical Essentials: Tickets, Transit & Scam Alerts

Tickets & Passport Booking

  • Price: Free. The mall, the terraces and the roof sculpture court cost nothing and need no booking; there is no ticket desk anywhere in the building.
  • How to Book: Nothing to book. Passport is not required to enter. The towers themselves (offices and the hotel) are closed to the public — the rooftop sculpture court on the podium is as high as visitors go, and its exact opening rules change with weather and events, so check at the lobby information desk rather than relying on published hours.
  • Opening Hours: The mall runs roughly 10:00–22:00 daily, with restaurants staying open later. The outdoor terraces close earlier in rain or high wind.

How to Get There

  • By Metro: Lines 1 and 2 to Wuyi Square Station, Exit 3 or 4 — you come up directly into the block. Line 1 to Huangxing Square Station also puts you two minutes from the podium’s south entrance. Everything here is flat paving; the building is fully step-free and wheelchair accessible.
  • Show Your Driver: 师傅，请带我到长沙国金中心 IFS，五一广场东侧，谢谢！

Inside Tips & Scam Alerts

  • The fake-brand sellers on the Huangxing Road pavement are not a grey area. Watches and bags offered from a mat are counterfeits, buying them is an infringement, and police do spot checks in this exact block. Keep walking.
  • “Photographers” on the roof terrace charge by the shot. There is no official photo service; agree a price first or use your own phone, and be aware the terrace gets crowded enough at sunset that tripods are impractical.
  • Eat elsewhere. The mall’s food floors are convenient but a 400 m walk south to Pozi Street gets you the same dishes, better, for less — and Huogongdian is on that street.
  • International cards work here, unlike most of the city: this is one of few places in Changsha where Visa and Mastercard are accepted at the counter without a Chinese payment app.
  • Lifts are zoned. The podium lifts only serve the mall; tower lifts are keycard-controlled. Ask at the desk rather than pressing buttons on the wrong bank.
  • Complaints: the 12345 hotline is free from any local phone and has English operators.
