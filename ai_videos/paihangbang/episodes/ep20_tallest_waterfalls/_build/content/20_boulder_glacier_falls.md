## WWD 描述（原文摘录，仅内部调研，不进成片）

> Boulder Glacier Falls is among the tallest waterfalls in Montana's Glacier National Park.  Draining from a small basin which harbors the remnants of the Boulder Glacier (which is most likely stagnant at this point), the stream produced tumbles down the massive headwall at the head of Bowman Creek's valley in a long chain of nearly continuous falls and cascades which have a cumulative drop of approximately 2,350 feet over a linear distance of about four-fifths of a mile.  Intermixed within the series of falls are several sections of talus cascades which break up the continuous bedrock falls, however most are fairly short lived and the stream appears to have scoured down to the bedrock for the majority of its descent to the valley floor.  As the Boulder Glacier shrinks and disappears, this waterfall is likely to become a seasonal feature.
