## WWD 描述（原文摘录，仅内部调研，不进成片）

> Døntefossen is a tall and prominent but lower volume waterfall found in Norway's famous Romsdalen.  Based on one-meter contour topographic maps and aerial imagery, the falls drop for a total of approximately 745 meters (2,445 feet) over a linear run of about 780 meters, broken up in three distinct sections.  The uppermost 225 meters of the fall consists of a continuous sliding cascade which descends over slabs of bedrock.  Immediately below the cascades is a much steeper horsetail type drop of approximately 200 meters, then after an additional 50-75 meters of cascades, the final distinct drop of about 250 meters plunges to the valley floor.  When viewed from the road across the river from the falls, the uppermost cascades are not visible due to the height of the cliffs, and only the final 500 meters or so of the falls will be visible (provided low clouds or fog do not obstruct it from view).
> The Døntelva is fed by about two-dozen small tarns and lakes perched on a shoulder of the valley, which are in turn fueled by the snowmelt of the mountain above.  Though the drainage basin is small and the stream will shrink considerably during the dry season once the snow has melted off, the numerous lakes above the falls ensure a fairly consistent flow feeding into the falls for the majority of the year (though due to the northern latitude, the falls will become frozen during the winter months).
> When we surveyed the falls in June of 2011, low clouds and rain prevented viewing more than the lowermost tier of the falls, and the inclement weather didn't allow us to take any pictures of the falls either, but we feel it presented itself well enough to afford us a judgment on its significance - it isn't a huge waterfall but neither is it minor.  Since nearby Mongefossen no longer flows regularly, Døntefossen in effect has become one of the best waterfalls in the valley and anyone visiting the Romsdalen area should certainly take a look.
> We revisited the falls in July of 2026.  The water was meager at best, but the fog and mist were high enough to afford us a clear, albeit low-volume look at the lower tier of the falls.
