## WWD 描述（原文摘录，仅内部调研，不进成片）

> Litldalen features some of the most spectacular cliffs in Norway, in many areas rising for more than 800 meters from the valley floor.  The sheer topography does not allow for much in the way of catchment atop their lofty summits, but in a few places several low volume waterfalls stream down from above.  Tågfossen is the tallest and most impressive of several such waterfalls found throughout the valley.  Fed by a small lake in a flat basin above Dalavatnet, the falls drop a total of 710 meters (2,330 feet) in a single delicate horsetail type fall which begins as a cascade and then reverts to a sheer plunge and then back to a veiling horsetail for its final descent.  Because of the small drainage area the falls are not expected to flow in the late summer months unless periods of prolonged rain are experienced.
> Tågfossen certainly isn’t unique in Norway as there are literally hundreds of such tall, low volume waterfalls found throughout the country.  What makes this one special though is its ease of access and the fact that you can stand almost directly beneath the falls (with a little effort it should be easy to scramble to the base of the falls to do exactly that).  Being so close to such a tall waterfall obviously compounds the natural foreshortening that takes place, but the impact of being so close to a waterfall over 700 meters tall is still quite humbling.
