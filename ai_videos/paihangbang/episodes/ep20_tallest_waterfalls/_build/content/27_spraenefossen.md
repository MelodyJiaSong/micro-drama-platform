## WWD 描述（原文摘录，仅内部调研，不进成片）

> Sprænefossen is a very tall but low volume and inconsequential multi-step waterfall found along the Osafjord in Ulvik.  The falls’ namesake stream drains from several modest tarns perched over 1,000 meters above the sea and upon reaching the edge of the fjord the stream skips, veils and plunges for a total of approximately 700 meters (2,295 feet) in a long chain of falls before ending on an alluvial fan and flowing more calmly into the sea.  Because the drainage area feeding into the falls is quite small, the volume of water flowing over the falls is rather low – even during periods of peak flow – and as a result the falls may not flow consistently throughout the year (and may even dry out entirely in the late summer months without consistent precipitation to replenish the stream flow).
> The official name of this waterfall is Sprænefossen, per Norgeskart.  We had not been able to find any information about whether this waterfall had a formal name for quite some time, and had previously referred to it as Balåifossen.
> When we surveyed the falls in June 2011, we were expecting a much more visually significant waterfall – not so much because of its great height (though our initial estimates turned out to be too generous by a considerable margin), but because many pictures we have seen showed a considerable volume of water present in the falls.  It’s very possible that the falls flow well as long as snow is melting in the basin above the falls, but without a consistent source of melt water the stream shrinks in volume quickly.  Even though the falls are easy to view, we left quite disappointed with this one and would not recommend that visitors to Norway seek it out unless they are already in the immediate vicinity (and even then it isn’t really worth the time).
