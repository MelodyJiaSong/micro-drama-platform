## WWD 描述（原文摘录，仅内部调研，不进成片）

> Isella Falls is one of the tallest waterfalls in Washington.  The falls occur along the meltwater streams originating in the Isella Glacier on the south face of Bonanza Peak, itself the tallest non-volcanic peak, and fifth tallest overall mountain in Washington State.  The falls begin as numerous streams of water sliding and veiling in parallel, slowly congealing into fewer and fewer channels, and finally culminating into a single stream before it reaches the bottom of the cascades at the valley floor.  Because the numerous streams emanating from the glacier are widespread and not well consolidated, it is difficult to pinpoint an elevation at which the waterfall could be determined to formally begin.  We have tentatively spotted the top of the waterfall at the 6,650 foot elevation, which would result in the falls having a total drop of approximately 2,500 feet from top to bottom.  It is entirely conceivable that the falls extend further up the mountain from that point, in which case Isella Falls could conceivably be not only Washington State's tallest waterfall, but potentially the tallest waterfall in the continental United States as well.
> The falls can be viewed from the Railroad Creek Trail for a considerable distance.  The base of the falls is located about one-tenth of a mile north of the trail and is accessible via a short and relatively brush free bushwhack from the trail.  Because of the immense height of the falls, only about half of the falls can be seen from its base.  More of the upper parts of the falls can be seen from trails above Lyman Lake further to the west.
